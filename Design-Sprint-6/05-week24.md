## 1. What We Are Doing

This week you send real applications, run a full interview loop, and finish with a final defense. You also write a 90-day plan and a final reflection. Week 24 completes the 27-week course, which includes week 0, weeks 1 to 24, week 12b and week 20b. The work is about evidence: your portfolio, your reasoning, and your honesty about gaps.

Task groups:
- Group 1: Application strategy and materials (`n/a` reading, `B4.1`, `B4.2`)
- Group 2: Send and connect (`B4.3`, `B4.4`)
- Group 3: Interview loop and defense (`n/a` mock loop, `n/a` final defense)
- Group 4: Plan and close (`B4.5`, `n/a` final reflection)

## 2. Why We Are Doing It

In India, product design roles get many applications without an introduction. Referrals may help, and a specific message may get read with more attention. A hiring manager checks your portfolio, resume, and LinkedIn for the same story. This week turns the work of the 27-week course into real applications, interview answers, and a plan you control.

For your project and portfolio, this week checks your work under interview questions. Your case studies, design system, accessibility evidence, and ethics position have to hold up under questions. The mock loop and final defense show you what to fix before a real interview.

## 3. What to Learn First

By the end of the week you can:
- Send an application that gets read
- Complete a full interview loop without collapsing in any one section
- Say honestly what you are still weak at after the 27-week course
- Keep improving without a program telling you what to do

Core ideas to review first:
- Referral versus an application without an introduction: what each route may add
- Applicant tracking system (ATS): software that stores and filters applications
- Fit over prestige: why a less famous team can be a better target
- Four-sentence cover note: what you do, why them, one relevant project, one link
- Application tracking: company, role, date, route, customisation, response
- Outreach with one specific question, not a job request
- Mock loop stages: recruiter screen, portfolio review, whiteboard, craft, behavioural, feedback discussion
- Placement readiness: Ready, Ready with a named gap, or Not yet
- Defense: reason out loud, use evidence, change your mind, say where knowledge ends
- 90-day plan: gaps, actions, dates, practice, review date
- Final reflection: name a task you can now do

### Group 3 - Interview loop and defense

#### Task - Mock interview loop (ID `n/a`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Run a full 3-hour interview loop with someone who has not been your reviewer. An external designer is ideal.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
You need practice in every stage and written feedback. Better here than in a real loop where you do not find out why you were rejected.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- The six stages and their timing
- The scorecard in `../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md` section 3
- Placement readiness outcome: Ready, Ready with a named gap, or Not yet

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
`../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md` section 3, your portfolio, and an interviewer who has not been your reviewer.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Book 3 hours with an interviewer who has not been your reviewer. Use an external designer if possible.
2. Send them the stage table and the scorecard.
3. Run the loop in this order:

| Stage | Time | What happens |
|---|---:|---|
| Recruiter screen | 20 min | Motivation, background, salary expectation |
| Portfolio review | 45 min | Two projects, with questions throughout |
| Whiteboard | 45 min | A prompt you have not seen |
| Craft interview | 30 min | Given a design, critique it and specify a fix |
| Behavioural | 30 min | Conflict, failure, and how you work |
| Feedback discussion | 10 min | Scored feedback, per stage |

4. Ask for a written score per stage using the scorecard in `../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md` section 3. The feedback should be honest and specific.
5. Ask for a placement-readiness outcome: Ready, Ready with a named gap, or Not yet.
6. Save the feedback in `students/UX{n}/week24/B4-mock-loop-feedback.md`.
7. Commit the file.

How to think about it: the loop is a test of reasoning, not a performance of perfection. Ask for the hard feedback now.

If you get stuck:
- If no external designer is free, ask a peer in Discord.
- If three continuous hours are not possible, split the six stages across sessions and keep all listed stage durations.
- Use the scorecard to keep feedback specific.

##### 6. What to Commit
- [ ] `students/UX{n}/week24/B4-mock-loop-feedback.md`
- [ ] A written score per stage
- [ ] A placement-readiness outcome
- [ ] Next actions from the feedback discussion
- [ ] Commit message: `B4 mock loop feedback`

#### Task - Final defense (ID `n/a`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Take a 90-minute final defense. It runs as an interview, not as a review. The whole 27-week course is in scope: week 0, weeks 1 to 24, week 12b and week 20b.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
You need to show that you can reason out loud, defend a decision with evidence, and say honestly where your knowledge ends.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- Portfolio walk with interruptions
- Whiteboard with a fresh prompt
- Craft numbers: spacing, contrast, type, motion, tokens
- Accessibility evidence
- Systems: tokens, components, governance
- Ethics position and the last time you tested it
- Reflection on what you are still bad at

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
`../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md` section 5, your portfolio, and your six reflections.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Book 90 minutes with your reviewer for the defense.
2. Send the reviewer the defense table below and the placement readiness criteria in `../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md` section 5.
3. Read the defense table before the session.
4. Bring your portfolio and two case studies.
5. Run the session in this order:

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---:|---|
| Portfolio walk | 20 min | Two case studies, with interruptions |
| Whiteboard | 25 min | A fresh prompt, out loud |
| Craft interrogation | 15 min | Numbers. Spacing, contrast, type, motion, tokens. |
| Accessibility | 10 min | Any criterion, any project, and how you verified it |
| Systems | 10 min | Tokens, components, governance |
| Ethics | 5 min | Your position, and the last time you tested it |
| Reflection | 5 min | What you are still bad at |

6. Ask for your placement-readiness outcome from `../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md` section 5.
7. Ask for the written list of what to do next.
8. If you take notes, add them to `students/UX{n}/week24/final-reflection.md`.

The pass condition is not perfection. It is that you can be given a problem, ask the right questions, reason out loud, defend a decision with evidence, change your mind when shown better evidence, and say honestly where your knowledge ends.

The two failure modes: bluffing an answer you do not have, and collapsing when challenged on something you do. Both are graded down. "I don't know, and here is how I would find out" is a passing answer. Inventing a number is not.

How to think about it: an honest gap with a method is stronger than a confident guess.

If you get stuck:
- Practice two 2-minute answers for your weakest project.
- Bring one piece of evidence for every claim.
- Ask in Discord for a hard question on accessibility or systems.

How to think about it: plan for two cases, with a job and without a job. Keep the practice small enough to do.
