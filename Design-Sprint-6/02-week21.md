## 1. What We Are Doing

This week you choose three projects and turn them into three case studies. A case study is a short story of a real project: the problem, the decisions, and the result. You plan all three before you write, then write, test and cut them.

Task groups:
- Group 1 - Choose and plan: B1.1, B1.2
- Group 2 - Write and test: B1.3, B1.4, B1.5
- Group 3 - Edit: B1.6
- Group 4 - Cohort day: F21.1, F21.2, F21.3

## 2. Why We Are Doing It

A working product designer in India is hired on evidence: what problem you solved, what you decided, what changed, and what you learned. Build 2 to 3 deep case studies that show your decisions and evidence; hiring preferences vary. This week turns your twenty weeks of work into proof for your portfolio and for interviews. It also prepares you for week 22, where you shape the full portfolio.

> This is new for most people. Go slowly. A clear case study is not decorated work. It is an argument with evidence.

## 3. What to Learn First

By the end of the week you can:
- Choose which of your projects is worth showing, on evidence rather than looks
- Write a case study that can be understood after 90 seconds of reading, and that holds up for a twenty-minute read
- Include a failure without undermining yourself
- Cut your own writing by a third

Core ideas to review first:
- A case study needs a hook, context, problem, one key finding, design decisions, a tradeoff, what testing changed, outcome, and what you would do differently.
- A hook is one or two sentences. It names a specific problem and a specific result.
- Evidence beats opinion. Numbers, quotes, test results and observed behaviour are evidence.
- One failure can make the work feel real. Do not hide every mistake.
- Screenshots need captions. An uncaptioned screenshot is decoration.
- Cutting 30% usually makes the argument easier to see.

#### Task - Structure each one (ID `B1.2`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing

Plan all three case studies before you write any of them. Decide the content of each section first.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It

A plan stops you from writing a tour of screens. It keeps each case study focused on the problem, decisions and result.

##### 3. What to Learn First

For each case study, review these parts:
- The hook
- Context
- The problem
- What you found
- The design
- A specific tradeoff
- What testing changed
- The outcome
- What you would do differently

##### 4. Where to Find Resources

Use your B1.1 selection file, your JR drafts, reviewer notes, and `06-case-study-structure.md`.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide

1. Open your B1.1 selection file.
2. For case study one, write the hook in one or two sentences. The hook says what the problem was and what changed.
3. Write the context in 100 words maximum: what, who for, your role, when, how long.
4. Write the problem with evidence. Use numbers if you have them.
5. Write the one finding that changed your direction. Not all findings. The one that mattered.
6. List the design decisions, not a tour of screens.
7. Name one specific tradeoff: what you chose against, and what it cost.
8. Describe one thing testing changed. Show one before and one after.
9. Report observed outcomes with evidence. If no outcome was measured, state that clearly and describe what you would measure next.
10. Write what you would do differently.
11. Make the answer real. Name one specific change and what it would fix.
12. Mark every screenshot you plan to use. Write the reason for each one.
13. Repeat steps 2 to 12 for case study two.
14. Repeat steps 2 to 12 for case study three.

Weak hook:
> "A UX case study exploring the redesign of a hospital appointment booking experience."

Why it is weak: it names a topic, not a problem, and it gives no result.

Now write your own hook. Answer these three prompts in one or two sentences:
- What was the specific problem, and who had it?
- What did you do about it?
- What result did you observe, and what evidence shows it?

Use only evidence you actually have. If you did not measure a result, say what you observed and how you observed it.

If you get stuck: read `06-case-study-structure.md` again. Search "UX case study hook problem result". Ask in Discord with your draft hook and what you tried.

How to think about it: if someone reads only the hook and looks at one image, they should still understand what you did.

### Group 3 - Edit

Learning note for this group: session 5, 45 minutes (the editing advice). Commit `students/UX{n}/week21/session5-learning.md` with the three cuts you will make. Commit message: `B1.6 session 5 learning note`.

#### Task - Shorten and edit (ID `B1.6`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing

Cut each case study by 30%. Check every claim. Fix every sentence you stumble over when reading aloud.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It

Longer writing does not get read. Unsupported claims damage trust. A tight case study is easier to skim and easier to defend in an interview.

##### 3. What to Learn First

Review the editing advice: cut adverbs, cut filler, and cut the first paragraph. Remove vague filler. Keep wording that accurately states uncertainty or limits in your evidence. "We tested with 5 people, so this is a small signal" is honest. "I felt that perhaps" is filler.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources

Use your three case study files from B1.3, B1.4 and B1.5, and your notes from the writing-for-the-web reading.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide

1. Open case study one.
2. Record the word count before editing.
3. Cut 30% of the words. Not 10%. Thirty.
4. Remove process description first.
5. Remove vague filler. Keep wording that accurately states uncertainty or limits in your evidence.
6. Remove any explanation of what a standard method is.
7. Remove every sentence that says what you were about to do rather than what you did.
8. Read the case study aloud.
9. Rewrite every sentence you stumble over.
10. Check every claim against something in your repository.
11. Remove or soften any claim you cannot support.
12. Record the word count after editing.
13. Repeat steps 1 to 12 for case study two.
14. Repeat steps 1 to 12 for case study three.
15. Save the edited versions in your B1.6 folder.

If you get stuck: read one paragraph aloud and stop at the first unclear sentence. Search "editing UX case study remove filler". Ask in Discord with before and after word counts and what you tried.

How to think about it: cutting is not losing work. It is making the argument easier to see.
