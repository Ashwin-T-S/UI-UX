## 2. Why We Are Doing It

In India, product design interviews often mix portfolio review, a whiteboard task, a live critique, behavioural questions and salary talk in one loop. Hiring teams look for clear reasoning, collaboration and judgment, not a perfect drawing. This week turns your 23 weeks of work into spoken evidence. It helps your capstone, your portfolio and your job applications.

## 3. What to Learn First

By the end of the week you can:
- Walk a case study in five minutes and in twenty, from the same material.
- Solve a design problem out loud, on a whiteboard, in forty-five minutes.
- Critique someone else's design in an interview without being either soft or unpleasant.
- Answer the question you are worst at without freezing.

Core ideas to review first:
- The question bank: each question has a note on what the interviewer is actually testing.
- Whiteboard assessment: constraints, assumptions, reasoning and how you respond to a changed instruction.
- The 45 minute sequence: clarify, frame, sketch different options, choose, detail, self-critique.
- STAR for behavioural answers: situation, task, action, result. Do not announce it.
- Critique with evidence: name what works, what fails, and what evidence you want.
- Salary: know a range, say a number, then stop talking.
- Figma file rule: use one file per sprint named `SPRINT-N UX{n}`, one page per week. Do not make three Figma files per sprint.

### Group 1 - Prepared answers

#### Task - Prepared answers (ID `B3.1`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Write answers to 40 questions from the bank. Then say them aloud and cut them in half. Cover process, a specific project, conflict, failure, accessibility, systems, ethics, and craft.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
Interviews test whether you can explain your judgment in a short time. Prepared answers help you stay specific under pressure.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Read `07-interview-question-bank.md` in full. Each question has a note on what the interviewer is testing. Know the difference between the surface question and the real question.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
`07-interview-question-bank.md`. Your own week 1 to week 22 files for real examples. Browser setup guide: `../04-Tools-Setup.md`.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Read the question bank note for each question before you write.
2. Pick 40 questions. Cover process, a specific project, conflict, failure, accessibility, systems, ethics, and craft.
3. Write a first answer for each one.
4. Say each answer out loud.
5. Cut each answer in half. A written answer is too long as a spoken one.
6. For every project question, add a specific number. Use a real result from your work.
7. For failure and conflict, choose real examples from your completed coursework. If none fits, describe a real event from another setting.
8. Mark the three questions you are worst at. For most people these are: why did you leave your last field, what is your weakness, and what would you have done if the business overruled you.
9. Drill those three more than the others.
10. Save your answers as markdown.

Checklist for a weakness answer:
- A real weakness, not a humblebrag.
- A specific event where it showed.
- The effect it had, including a cost.
- The action you took about it.
- The difficulty that is still there.

A strong answer names a real fault, gives a specific instance with a cost, describes a concrete correction, and admits the correction is effortful. Every part is necessary.

How to think about it: A good answer is short, specific, and shows a trade-off. If it has no number and no choice, it is still too general.

If you get stuck: read the question note again. Search `design interview answer specific number`. Ask in Discord with your draft answer and what you tried.

### Group 2 - Whiteboard exercises one and two

#### Task - Whiteboard exercise one (ID `B3.2`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Run one 45 minute whiteboard exercise, timed and recorded. Prompt: design a way for a delivery rider to report that an address does not exist.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
The interviewer scores how you think, how you ask about constraints, and how you respond to a changed instruction. A plain sketch with clear reasoning beats a polished drawing with no questions.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Learn the 45 minute sequence: clarify, frame, sketch different options, choose, detail, self-critique. Ask before you draw.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
The whiteboard method in this week. Read it first, 45 minutes, and use your notes for `session2-learning.md`. Your free recorder. Browser setup guide: `../04-Tools-Setup.md`.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Ask in Discord for a practice partner for this exercise. The partner plays the interviewer.
2. If you cannot find a partner, do the exercise alone and write your assumptions down as you go.
3. Set a timer for 45 minutes.
4. Start recording.
5. Clarify, 5 min: ask who the user is.
6. Ask what the business context is.
7. Ask which platform it is on.
8. Ask what is out of scope.
9. If you are alone, say out loud that your answers are assumptions and are not confirmed.
10. Frame, 5 min: state the problem in one sentence.
11. Ask for agreement on the framing.
12. Sketch different options, 10 min: sketch three approaches.
13. Describe each approach in one sentence.
14. Choose, 5 min: pick one.
15. Say why it wins against the criteria you clarified.
16. Detail, 15 min: sketch the main flow.
17. Say what you are choosing at each screen.
18. Critique yourself, 5 min: name the weaknesses.
19. Name what you would test.
20. Name what you would do next.
21. Talk continuously through the whole exercise.
22. Narrate even the uncertainty. Example: "I'm not sure whether this should be one screen or two, so I'm going to sketch one and note the split as an option."
23. Stop recording.
24. Watch it back.

How to think about it: For this practice exercise, ask clarifying questions before sketching. The questions and the framing are the work.

If you get stuck: watch your recording at 2x. Search `whiteboard design interview clarifying questions`. Ask in Discord with the minute where you got stuck and your sketch.

#### Task - Critique performance (ID `B3.5`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Choose one public product page to critique. Do not open personal profile screens, log in or submit anything. You then critique it live, in ten minutes. This is a standard interview exercise. Record it.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
Interviewers watch how you talk about their product, and by extension how you would talk about a colleague's work. They want specific, fair critique, not contempt.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Use this structure: what it is trying to do, what works and why, what does not work and why, what you would change first and what evidence you would want, what you would need to know before you were confident. When you talk about contrast, measure it and record the text size. Normal text needs at least 4.5:1 for AA. Large text needs at least 3:1.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
The critique method in this task. Your free recorder. Browser setup guide: `../04-Tools-Setup.md`.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Choose one public product page to critique. Do not open personal profile screens, log in or submit anything.
2. Set a timer for 10 minutes.
3. Start recording.
4. Say what the product is trying to do.
5. Say what works and why.
6. Say what does not work and why.
7. Say what you would change first and what evidence you would want.
8. Say what you would need to know before you were confident.
9. Be specific and fair. Example: "The secondary text that carries the price measures 3:1 at 14 pixels. Normal text needs at least 4.5:1 for AA, so this fails AA." That is a critique. "It looks cluttered" is not.
10. Do not speak disrespectfully about the product or the team.
11. Stop recording.
12. Watch it back.

How to think about it: Critique the work, not the person. Name something genuine that works before you name what fails.

If you get stuck: write the five structure lines on paper. Search `design critique interview specific evidence`. Ask in Discord with your one paragraph critique and what you tried.

### Group 4 - Portfolio walkthrough and peer critique

#### Task - Portfolio walkthrough (ID `B3.6`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Record a twenty minute walkthrough of one case study. Then record a five minute version of the same one. Then watch both back.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
The five minute version is harder and is the one you will often be asked for. Interview formats vary. Practise both lengths so you can adapt to the time requested.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Know your case study decisions: the problem, the evidence, the trade-offs, the outcome and the one thing you would do next.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Your case study in Figma, or in Penpot if the Figma file limit blocks you (check the free limit before use). Use one file per sprint named `SPRINT-N UX{n}`, one page per week. Setup guide: `../04-Tools-Setup.md`. Your free recorder.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Choose one case study.
2. Record a twenty minute walkthrough.
3. Record a five minute version of the same case study.
4. Watch both back.
5. Count your filler words.
6. Note every place you explained your process instead of your reasoning.
7. Re-record whichever version is worse.
8. Join the 90 minute peer critique. Peers run a portfolio review on you and ask hostile questions.
9. Answer these questions in the critique: "how do you know that?", "what would you have done if the research said the opposite?", and "which part of this are you least confident about?"

How to think about it: The five minute version is a selection problem, not a speed problem. Keep the decision points and cut the process tour.

If you get stuck: cut one slide or one story. Search `5 minute portfolio walkthrough`. Ask in Discord with your outline and what you tried.

### Group 5 - Behavioural and negotiation

#### Task - Behavioural and negotiation (ID `B3.7`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Prepare behavioural answers and the salary conversation. Also prepare five questions to ask the interviewer.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
Behavioural questions test how you work with people. Salary talk tests whether you can stay calm and clear. Both are part of the interview loop.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Use STAR: situation, task, action, result. It is a structure, not a script. Do not announce it.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
`07-interview-question-bank.md` for behavioural questions. Research the salary range for your level and location locally, and write the source in your notes.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Prepare answers for: a disagreement with an engineer.
2. Prepare an answer for: a time you were wrong.
3. Prepare an answer for: a time you had to ship something you were not happy with.
4. Prepare an answer for: how you handle feedback you disagree with.
5. Prepare an answer for: what you do when you have no research budget.
6. Use STAR for each answer, but do not announce it.
7. Research the actual salary range for your level and location.
8. Practise saying a number and then stopping talking. The stopping is the part everyone gets wrong.
9. Prepare five of your own questions. They should be questions whose answers would actually change whether you accepted.
10. Practise all answers aloud.

Topic prompts for your five questions:
- Why people leave this team, and what that says about the role.
- How the team handled the last design decision that was overruled.
- How design work is chosen: research-led or requested by stakeholders.
- What happens when accessibility conflicts with a deadline.
- What the role is expected to deliver by month three.

Write five questions in your own words, tied to your own job criteria, meaning what you need from a role. Do not copy these lines. Each question must be one whose answer would change whether you accepted the job.

How to think about it: The salary number is a range with a reason, not a wish. Say it, then stop.

If you get stuck: write one STAR answer in four lines. Search `STAR behavioural interview design`. Ask in Discord with your answer and what you tried.
