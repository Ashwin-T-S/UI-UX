## 1. What We Are Doing

This week you run your own four-week project. You write the brief, the task list, the research plan, and the evidence. You pass an evidence gate before design starts.

- Group 1 - Brief and scope: K1.1, K1.2
- Group 2 - Research: K1.3, K1.4

- Group 4 - System, fundamentals, and AI lab: S21, F17.1, F17.2, F17.3, L-AI5

## 2. Why We Are Doing It

A working product designer in India often gets a messy brief and no template. You must scope the work, research the problem, and prove the problem before you design. This week matters for your capstone because design cannot start on a weak problem. It matters for your portfolio because employers look for scoped work with real evidence and a clear problem statement.

## 3. What to Learn First

By the end of the week you can:

- Write your own scoped task list for four weeks and defend the scope.
- Run research on your own project without a template telling you how.
- Pass an evidence gate on work nobody assigned you.
- Justify what you chose to learn this week.

Core ideas to review first:

- Domain knowledge before design theory. You cannot frame a problem in a domain you do not understand.
- Method selection. Choose research methods for your questions, not from habit.
- Real participants. Minimum three. Five is better.
- Synthesis with denominators and confidence levels.
- Problem statement shape: `[user] cannot [job] because [obstacle], which costs [user cost] and [business cost].`
- Scope control: critical path, cut list, and never-cut list.
- Metrics: primary, input, guardrail.
- Evidence gate: no design until the evidence passes.
- System governance: do not bypass your own Sprint 4 process.
- AI feature lab: L-AI5. Week 20b later extends it into the Agent UX lab.

Learning summary format from now on. You still commit a learning summary for every session:

1. **What I learned** - the source, and specifically what part.
2. **Why I needed it this week** - tied to a task you are doing now.
3. **What I did with it** - the specific decision it changed.
4. **What I chose not to learn** - and why it waits.

Point 4 is the new one. In a four-week project with a fixed deadline, choosing what to remain ignorant of is a real skill.

### Group 1 - Brief and scope

Before K1.1, do the Day 1 LEARN block. Write `students/UX{n}/week17/session1-learning.md` with the four-section format.

#### Task - Your project brief (ID `K1.1`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Write your own project brief. It has six sections: context; users and how you know; the problem as you understand it now; constraints as fixed, technical, or assumed; what is out of scope; what you do not yet know.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
You cannot frame a problem in a domain you do not understand. The out-of-scope section is graded. A four-week project that excludes nothing will not finish.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- Day 1 LEARN is domain knowledge, not design theory.
- Examples: how appointment scheduling works in a district hospital, platform payment terms, or apprenticeship eligibility rules.
- The six brief sections.
- Constraint types: fixed, technical, assumed.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Use the source your project needs. No fixed chapter. If you need the writing tool setup, see `../04-Tools-Setup.md`.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Open a new markdown file at `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-1-brief.md`.
2. Write the context in 2 to 3 sentences.
3. Describe the users.
4. Name the source for your user description.
5. Write the problem as you currently understand it.
6. List your constraints.
7. Mark each constraint as fixed, technical, or assumed.
8. Write the out-of-scope list.
9. Give a reason for each out-of-scope item.
10. List what you do not know.
11. Read the Done when list and fix gaps.

If you get stuck:
- Re-read the six section names under "1. What We Are Doing".
- Search keyword "project brief scope".
- Ask in Discord with your draft out-of-scope list and what you tried.

How to think about it: A scope is a promise. Name what you will not do so the rest can be trusted.

##### 6. What to Commit
- [ ] `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-1-brief.md`
- [ ] All six sections
- [ ] Users described with a source for the description
- [ ] Constraints classified into the three types
- [ ] Out of scope is specific and justified, not vague
- [ ] What you do not know is honest and listed
- [ ] Commit message: `K1.1: write project brief`

Allowed: .md, PNG/JPG under 2 MB, PDF under 10 MB, small .html/.css/.js files, or a view-only Figma or Penpot link inside .md. No videos or GIFs.

#### Task - Your scoped task list (ID `K1.2`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Write your own task list for the four weeks. Give every task an ID, a time, a dependency, and a definition of done. Mark the critical path and the cut list.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
A four-week project with no cut list will overrun. You need to know what slips together and what you drop first.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- Definition of done.
- Dependencies and critical path.
- Time budget: 4 weeks x 5 days x 4 hours = 80 hours.
- A cut list in order.
- A never-cut list with a reason.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Use your K1.1 brief and your domain notes. No fixed chapter. If you need the commit steps, see `../10-GitHub-For-Designers.md`.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Open `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-2-task-list.md`.
2. Write every task for the four weeks. Use your own IDs and structure.
3. For each task, write what it is.
4. For each task, write when it happens.
5. For each task, write what it depends on.
6. For each task, write what "done" means.
7. Mark the critical path. If one task slips, which others slip with it?
8. Mark what you cut.
9. Mark what you would cut next if you lost time.
10. Total the hours.
11. If the total is over 80 hours, cut now, not in week 20.
12. Check that each cut has a project-specific reason and your never-cut list protects research and accessibility.

If you get stuck:
- Re-read step 6 and your total hours.
- Search keyword "critical path task list".
- Ask in Discord with your total hours and your top three cut items.

How to think about it: Cutting is not failure. It is how a fixed deadline stays honest.

##### 6. What to Commit
- [ ] `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-2-task-list.md`
- [ ] Every task listed with a definition of done
- [ ] Dependencies mapped, critical path marked
- [ ] Total hours calculated and within budget

### Evidence gate - Day 5 afternoon

You present. Nothing proceeds to design until this passes.

**How it runs:**
- Your reviewer is the person assigned to you in Discord. If you do not know who that is, ask in the week 17 channel.
- Post your evidence links in the Discord week 17 channel in one message, and tag your reviewer. Link K1-2, K1-4, K1-5, K1-6 and K1-7.
- The reviewer replies in that thread with either a pass or a list of required changes.
- Copy that reply into `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-7-metrics.md`, under a heading "Evidence gate", and commit it. This is where the pass or the required changes live.

**What is checked:**
- Real participants, properly consented and anonymised
- Findings with denominators and confidence levels
- A problem statement traceable to evidence
- Metrics with a guardrail
- A scope that is credible for three remaining weeks
- Assumed constraints identified

**If it fails,** use the weekend and the start of week 18 to address the reviewer's changes. Resubmit in Discord. Start design only after the reviewer records a pass.
