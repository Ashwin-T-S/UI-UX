## 1. What We Are Doing

This week you design the full product flow for Sprint 5. You use the standards from Sprints 1 to 4 without a reminder. You finish with a craft gate and a full accessibility pass.

The task groups are:
- Group 1 - Day 1: K2.1, K2.2
- Group 2 - Day 2: K2.3
- Group 3 - Day 3: K2.4, K2.5
- Group 4 - Day 4: K2.6, K2.7, critique
- Group 5 - Day 5: K2.8, craft gate
- Group 6 - Day 6: S22, F18.1, F18.2, F18.3, L-PR4

## 2. Why We Are Doing It

In a working product design job in India, a manager gives you a problem and a date. Nobody restates the quality bar. You must remember real content, states, contrast, decision records and focus indicators.

For your portfolio, this week shows a complete flow under time pressure. It shows you can hold a standard, not only make a few pretty screens.

## 3. What to Learn First

By the end of the week you can:
- Take a problem statement to a complete designed flow in five days
- Hold the Sprint 1 to 4 standards without anyone reminding you what they are
- Pass a craft gate on accessibility and system compliance
- Know which of your own standards you were tempted to drop under time pressure

Core ideas to review first:
- IA (information architecture): how content is grouped and named.
- Mid-fidelity: screens with real layout and real content, but not final visual polish.
- High fidelity: close to final visual design.
- A decision record: a short note of what you chose, why, and what you rejected.
- WCAG 2.2 AA: the accessibility standard level you are targeting this week.
- APG: WAI ARIA Authoring Practices Guide. It explains keyboard and screen reader behavior for common patterns.
- A craft gate: a review that checks quality standards before you move on.

Standards that are live this week, without being restated:
1. Real content instead of lorem
2. Empty, loading, and error states
3. Contrast measured rather than assumed
4. Decision records written at the time rather than reconstructed on Day 5
5. Focus indicators on custom components

If you catch yourself about to drop one, log it in your decision record as a deliberate choice with a reason. A logged shortcut is a decision. An unlogged one is a failure.

### Group 1 - Day 1: Structure and flows

Day 1 starts with 75 minutes of self-directed learning on what your IA problem needs. Save your summary as `students/UX{n}/week18/session1-learning.md`. Then commit it with the message `week18 add session 1 learning`.

#### Task - Structure (ID `K2.1`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Write your information architecture. Justify it with your research, not with convention. If you have a hierarchy, say why it is that hierarchy and what you rejected.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
A structure that comes from research reduces navigation errors and rework. In a portfolio, it shows you can explain a decision, not only draw a menu.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Review IA, card sorting, and task analysis. If your project has a complex taxonomy, review how card sorting turns into groups. If it has a difficult flow, review task analysis.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Use your research notes from Sprints 1 and 2. Use the search keyword "information architecture card sorting task analysis". Open Figma or Penpot for your map. Setup guide: `../04-Tools-Setup.md`.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Open your research notes.
2. List the main tasks your users need to do.
3. Group the content by those tasks.
4. Choose the hierarchy you will use.
5. Write why you chose it.
6. Write what you rejected and why.
7. Save the file.

How to think about it: If you cannot say why, the structure is not ready. "It is common" is not a reason.

If you get stuck: Read your research notes again. Search "information architecture card sorting task analysis". Ask in Discord with your outline and what you tried.

#### Task - Flows (ID `K2.2`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Design every flow the scope includes. Include the happy path and the failure paths.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It

##### 3. What to Learn First
Review task analysis and failure paths. A failure path is what the user sees when something goes wrong or cannot continue.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide

3. List the failure points in that flow.

5. Repeat for every flow.
6. Mark which research note supports each flow.
7. Save the file.

How to think about it: Design the failure first. It shows where the real work is.

### Group 5 - Day 5: Accessibility pass and craft gate

#### Task - Accessibility pass (ID `K2.8`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
Do a design audit of the WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA criteria. This is a design audit, not a full conformance test. Mark every criterion as pass, fail, not applicable, or "needs implementation testing". Give evidence for each pass. Give a reason for each "not applicable". Give a fix plan and a fix date for each fail.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
Accessibility is a quality gate, not a final extra. Evidence makes your work defensible in a review and in a portfolio.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Review WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA. A design audit covers what the prototype can show. Some criteria need working code or assistive technology tests, so they cannot be verified in a screen design. Mark those "needs implementation testing". A pass needs evidence, not a feeling. A "not applicable" needs a reason. A fail needs a fix date.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Open the W3C WCAG 2.2 Quick Reference at https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/. Use the APG page for your pattern. Search "WCAG 2.2 AA audit pass fail evidence".

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Open the WCAG 2.2 Quick Reference.
2. Make a table with one row per Level A and AA criterion.
3. Mark each row pass, fail, not applicable, or needs implementation testing.
4. Write evidence for each pass.
5. Write a reason for each not applicable.
6. Write a fix plan for each fail.
7. Set a fix date for each fail.
8. Check contrast values and record them.
9. Check the keyboard path.
10. Save the file.

How to think about it: A pass without evidence is a guess. Write what you tested and where. If a criterion needs working code or a screen reader test, say so instead of guessing.

If you get stuck: Read the quick reference again. Search "WCAG 2.2 AA audit evidence". Ask in Discord with the criterion and what you tried.
