## 1. What We Are Doing
This week you finish the capstone and package it for handoff. You check accessibility, present the work, and defend decisions from your work through week 20. You also write the reflection that closes Sprint 5.

- Group 1 - Day 1: Finalise (K4.1)
- Group 2 - Day 2: Handoff and decisions (K4.2, K4.3)
- Group 3 - Day 3: Accessibility conformance (K4.4)
- Group 4 - Day 4: Presentation and rehearsal (K4.5)
- Group 5 - Day 5: External presentation and defense
- Group 6 - Day 6: Reflection and job readiness (P20.1, F20.1, F20.2, F20.3, F20.4, JR5)

## 2. Why We Are Doing It
In a real product design job in India, you do not hand over a folder of screens. You hand over a spec an engineer can build from, a clear accessibility record, and a story a manager can repeat. This week trains that. It also gives your portfolio a strong ending: a shipped capstone, a clean decision trail, and a presentation you can reuse in interviews.

## 3. What to Learn First
By the end of the week you can:
- Ship a complete, specified, accessible design
- Present twenty minutes of work to someone who has never seen it
- Defend any decision from any of the last twenty weeks
- Say what you would do differently with the whole thing

Core ideas to review first:
- Final means both themes, every state, no TODO, no TBD in the design file.
- The consistency pass looks only for mismatches.
- The handoff spec passes one test: an engineer who never spoke to you could build it.
- The decision log is honest when each answer, yes or no, is explained with evidence.
- The conformance report needs evidence, not assertions.
- The presentation opens with the problem, not your process.
- The defense checks whether twenty weeks of learning stuck.
- Figma rule: use one Figma file per sprint named `SPRINT-N UX{n}`, with one page per week. Do not use three Figma files per sprint. If the Starter limit blocks you, use Penpot backup. Setup: `../04-Tools-Setup.md`.

### Group 1 - Day 1: Finalise

#### Task - Finalise every screen (ID `K4.1`)
##### 1. What We Are Doing
Finish all capstone screens, both themes, every state. Include week 19 changes and injection responses. Run one consistency pass. The injection is the surprise change added in week 19.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
A shipped file with mixed empty states or spacing looks unfinished. This pass is what makes the design feel professional.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- What "final" means for each screen.
- How to spot two treatments for the same case.
- How to move open items into the spec as open questions.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources

- Your state list from week 2.
- Figma setup: `../04-Tools-Setup.md` (Figma section).

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Open your sprint file `SPRINT-N UX{n}` in Figma or the Penpot backup, then go to the week 20 page.
2. Check each screen in both themes.
3. Add every missing state.
4. Apply every week 19 change.
5. Apply every response to the injection.
6. Do the consistency pass: look only for mismatches.
7. Log each fix in a short list.
8. Resolve each TODO or TBD, or move it to the spec open questions.
9. Walk the prototype end to end, including failure paths.

If you get stuck: read the consistency pass step again. Search "design consistency pass checklist". Ask in Discord with a screenshot and what you tried.

### Group 3 - Day 3: Accessibility conformance

#### Task - Accessibility conformance report (ID `K4.4`)
##### 1. What We Are Doing
Write a WCAG 2.2 AA conformance report for the capstone. Mark every criterion pass, fail, not applicable (N/A), or not verified; requires a build. Add evidence.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
Accessibility claims need proof. A design-stage report also shows that you know the limits of Figma checks.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA success criteria.
- What counts as evidence for a pass.
- How to mark what a design prototype cannot check.
- How to write a fail with severity, impact, fix, and date.
- How to justify N/A honestly.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
- https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/
- Your week 4 and week 12 accessibility notes.
- Your final screens and prototype.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Open the WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA success criteria.
2. For each criterion, mark pass, fail, not applicable (N/A), or not verified; requires a build.
3. For every pass, add evidence: a contrast measurement or a keyboard walkthrough note from the design file.
4. Record each specified announcement as a requirement awaiting an implementation test. Do not count it as proof on its own.
5. For every fail, add severity, who it affects, the fix, and the date.
6. For every N/A, write why it does not apply.
7. For every not verified, write what a build test would check.
8. Add a section on what you could not verify in Figma and would need a build to confirm.
9. Check that no pass is only an assertion.

If you get stuck: read the evidence step again. Search "WCAG 2.2 AA conformance report template". Open https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/. Ask in Discord with one criterion and the evidence you have.

##### 6. What to Commit
- [ ] `students/UX{n}/week20/K4-4-conformance.md`
- [ ] Every WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA success criterion addressed.
- [ ] Evidence attached to every pass
- [ ] Specified announcements recorded as requirements awaiting implementation tests
- [ ] Criteria that cannot be checked in a design prototype marked "not verified; requires a build"
- [ ] Every fail has severity, impact, fix, and date
- [ ] Every N/A justified
- [ ] The limits of design-stage verification stated honestly
- [ ] Commit message: `K4.4 accessibility conformance report`
- [ ] Allowed: .md, PDF under 10 MB, PNG/JPG under 2 MB.

### Group 4 - Day 4: Presentation and rehearsal

#### Task - Presentation (ID `K4.5`)
##### 1. What We Are Doing
Build a twenty-minute presentation for someone who has never seen the project. Use the five-part structure. Rehearse it out loud, timed.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
A good design can fail in a short presentation. This is also the story you will reuse in portfolio reviews and interviews.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- The problem, 3 min, with evidence.
- What you found, 4 min, the finding that changed your direction.
- The design, 8 min, walked and shown.
- What testing changed, 3 min, one before and after.
- What you would do next, 2 min, including what is still wrong.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
- Your capstone files and research notes.
- Your testing notes.
- A timer.
- Figma setup: `../04-Tools-Setup.md` (Figma section).

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Create the folder `K4-5-presentation/`.
2. Build the opening: the problem, 3 min, with evidence.
3. Add what you found, 4 min, the finding that changed your direction.
4. Add the design walk, 8 min.
5. Show the design, do not describe it.
6. Include the failure states in the walk.
7. Add what testing changed, 3 min, one before and after.
8. Add what you would do next, 2 min, including what is still wrong.
9. Remove process diagrams.
10. Check every user claim has evidence.
11. Add at least one thing that did not work.
12. Rehearse out loud with a timer.

How to think about it: The reviewer has not seen the project. The first three minutes must make the problem clear. If you start with process, you lose them.

If you get stuck: read the rules again. Search "20 minute design presentation structure". Ask in Discord with your outline and timing.

##### 6. What to Commit
- [ ] `students/UX{n}/week20/K4-5-presentation/`
- [ ] Twenty minutes, timed and rehearsed out loud
- [ ] Opens with the problem, not the process
- [ ] Every user claim traceable to evidence
- [ ] Failure states shown
- [ ] At least one thing that did not work, presented as such
- [ ] Ends with what is still wrong
- [ ] Commit message: `K4.5 presentation`
- [ ] Allowed: .md, PDF under 10 MB, PNG/JPG under 2 MB, view-only Figma or Penpot link inside a .md file.

### Group 5 - Day 5: External presentation and defense

#### Event - Presentation to external reviewer (20 minutes plus questions)
To an external reviewer. Someone who has not seen the project and does not know the program.

To find a reviewer: post in Discord and ask for a volunteer. Say what the project is in one line, the date and time, and that it is 20 minutes plus questions.

If no external reviewer is free, use the fallback: a designer from another group who has not seen your project or your presentation. Ask them not to open your files before the session.

Assessed on: whether the problem is clear in the first three minutes; whether the design is shown rather than described; whether claims have evidence; whether the answers to questions are honest; whether they would hire you off this. No commit. This is the delivery of K4.5.

#### Event - Defense (75 minutes)
Your work through week 20 is in scope.

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Problem and evidence | 10 min | The problem, the research, the confidence levels |
| Design walk | 15 min | Every screen, every state, from memory |
| Craft | 10 min | Spacing, type, colour, contrast, with numbers |
| Accessibility | 10 min | The conformance report, and the fails |
| Systems | 10 min | Your tokens, your components, your governance |
| Ethics | 5 min | Your position, and the problem in your own design |
| Cross-sprint | 10 min | Anything from weeks 1 to 20 |
| Alternative | 5 min | A constraint card |

The cross-sprint section is where your work through week 20 is actually tested. It might be a Sprint 1 contrast question, a Sprint 2 sampling question, a Sprint 3 easing question, or a Sprint 4 versioning question. There is no revision list, because the point is whether it stuck.

Sprint 5 constraint cards: the primary user group is removed from scope; the budget is halved and you must cut half the scope; the platform changes from mobile to SMS-only; a legal requirement adds a mandatory consent step to your core flow; you must hand this over tomorrow and are unavailable afterwards.

How to think about it: The defense is not a memory test of slide numbers. It is a test of whether you can reason from evidence under pressure.

No commit.
