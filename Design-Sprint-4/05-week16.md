## 1. What We Are Doing

## 2. Why We Are Doing It

A working product designer in India often joins a team with several products, fast deadlines, and a design system that people ignore. The job is not only to build components. The job is to make the system easier to use than the workaround. This week gives you proof for your portfolio: real adoption numbers, a migration plan, a diagnosis, and a defense.

## 3. What to Learn First

By the end of the week you can:
- Measure adoption with something better than a feeling
- Diagnose why a team forked your component instead of asking
- Write a migration guide someone actually completes
- Defend a system decision to the team it inconveniences

Core ideas to review first:
- One possible cause of low adoption is that using the system takes longer than the workaround when someone has a deadline.
- Adoption metrics need a guardrail, because numbers can be gamed.
- Migration order matters: tokens before components, low-risk screens before high-traffic screens.
- Interviewing people about your own work is hard. Do not defend. Find out what they expected.
- Non-adoption has different causes: discovery, documentation, gaps, speed, and disagreement. Each cause has a different owner.
- A health dashboard that hides bad numbers is marketing.
- A system lives between too strict and too loose. Standardisation and autonomy need balance.

### Group 1 - Measure and migrate

#### Task - Adoption measurement plan (ID `A1.1`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You define what adoption means for your system and choose numbers you could collect.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
Without a measure, adoption is an opinion. A guardrail stops you from celebrating numbers that are gamed.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- Adoption is a behaviour, not a feeling.
- Good metrics are collectable, repeatable, and tied to a trigger.
- A guardrail metric catches fake success.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Use the Day 1 sources: Kholmatova Ch. 8, a design system survey, and a Figma or Zeroheight article on adoption measurement.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Define what adoption means for your system. Be specific. "The team likes it" is not a measure.
2. Choose four or more metrics you can collect by hand, with free tools, this week.
3. Copy this empty table into your file and fill one row per metric:

| Metric | How you collect it | Frequency | Trigger for action |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |

4. Your Figma Starter plan gives you one file per sprint and no libraries. So count components by hand in a sample of frames inside your sprint file. Record how many frames you sampled. If you used Penpot, count in your Penpot file and name it.
5. For each metric, write the number that would trigger action, and why that number.
6. Write a guardrail: what result would show the metric is being gamed, and why. Choose this yourself and explain it.
7. Write what you cannot measure, the proxy you will use instead, and why the proxy is fair.

If you get stuck: read the Day 1 source again. Search "design system adoption metrics with guardrail". Ask in Discord with a screenshot and what you tried.

#### Task - Migration guide (ID `A1.2`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You write a guide for moving one product onto your system.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
An underestimated migration gets abandoned halfway. That leaves the product worse than not starting.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- Order matters. Tokens first, because components consume tokens.
- Start with low-risk screens, and with small components used inside larger ones (leaf components), so the team builds confidence.
- The section on what to do when there is no matching component (no-equivalent) decides whether the guide gets used.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Use the Day 2 sources: two published design system migration accounts, usually company engineering blogs.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Pick one of the three products. Write the guide for moving it onto your system.
2. Write six sections: what changes, what breaks, the order to do it in, the effort estimate per stage, how to verify each stage, and what to do when a screen has no matching component.
3. Fill this empty stage table for your chosen product:

| Stage | What changes | Why this order | Effort estimate | How you verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 |  |  |  |  |
| 2 |  |  |  |  |
| 3 |  |  |  |  |
| 4 |  |  |  |  |

4. Give a reason for each position in your order.
5. Give an honest effort estimate per stage, and say what you based it on.
6. Write the verification method per stage, and the result that tells you the stage is done.
7. Write the no-equivalent section. Give the team a path that does not involve waiting for you.

### Group 2 - Interview and diagnose

#### Task - Adoption interviews (ID `A1.3`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You interview three peers who have used your components.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
You need to hear what people expected, not what you intended. This is the hardest interview to run without leading.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- Your bias is at its maximum because you are interviewing about your own work.
- Do not defend. Do not explain what you intended.
- Ask what they did instead when the system did not have what they needed.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Use the Day 3 source: your Sprint 2 interviewing notes and your R4.2 self-critique.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Interview three peers who have used your components. Use the Sprint 2 protocol.
2. Do not defend your decisions. Do not explain what you intended.
3. When they describe something as confusing, ask what they expected instead.
4. Ask what they did when the system did not have what they needed. That answer is the whole finding.
5. Ask what they would fork if they had a deadline tomorrow.
6. Write notes the same day.

If you get stuck: re-read your Sprint 2 notes. Search "how to interview users about your own design without leading". Ask in Discord with one transcript line and what you tried.

How to think about it: your goal is to learn, not to win. If you feel the need to explain, write the explanation down for yourself after the interview.

#### Task - Diagnosis (ID `A1.4`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You find the actual cause for every case where someone did not use the system.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
Each cause has a different fix. Only one cause is about design. The rest are documentation, discovery, gap, or process problems.

##### 3. What to Learn First
- Honest categories: they did not know it existed, they could not find it, the documentation did not answer their question, it did not do what they needed, it was faster to build their own, or they disagreed with the design.
- The first three are documentation and discovery problems. The fourth is a gap. The fifth is a process problem.
- Rank by reach, not by how interesting the problem is.
- Your own work may be part of the cause. Look at your notes before you decide. If the notes do not show it, say so.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
Use your A1.3 interview notes and your A1.1 adoption metrics.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. List every non-adoption case from your interviews.
2. Put each case into one honest category.
3. For each case, check what your own work contributed. Was the component hard to find? Was the documentation unclear? Was the name wrong? Write your contribution, or write "no evidence found" if your notes do not show one.
4. For each cause, name the fix.
5. Name who owns the fix.
6. Rank the causes by how many people they affect.

If you get stuck: read your interview notes again. Search "design system adoption diagnosis categories". Ask in Discord with your cause table and what you tried.

How to think about it: "they should have asked" is not a diagnosis. Ask what made the workaround rational.

##### 6. What to Commit
- [ ] File: `students/UX{n}/week16/A1-4-diagnosis.md`
- [ ] Every non-adoption case categorised
- [ ] Your own contribution checked for every case, with "no evidence found" where your notes do not show one

- [ ] Ranked by reach

### Group 4 - Defend and reflect

Defense session - 60 minutes, no task ID.

How to run it in a self-study group:
1. Ask one peer in Discord to be your reviewer. Book a 60 minute call, or swap written feedback in a thread. You review their defense, they review yours.
2. Share your screen. Walk through the six parts below. Your peer keeps time and writes notes.
3. Ask your peer to note, for each part: what worked, what to change, and any claim you made without evidence.
4. Copy the feedback into `students/UX{n}/week16/defense-feedback.md` and commit it.
5. If no peer is free this week, record a Loom walkthrough (free plan, check the free limit before use) and post it in Discord for written feedback. Keep the feedback in the same file.

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Token architecture | 10 min | Three layers from memory, with real examples |
| Component API | 10 min | One component's API, every property justified |
| Governance | 10 min | Who decides what, and how something gets rejected |
| Deprecation | 10 min | What you removed, how, and who it inconvenienced |
| Adoption | 10 min | Your real numbers, including the bad ones, and your diagnosis |
| Alternative | 10 min | A constraint card is drawn |

The adoption section is where most people lose marks. The failure mode is defending the system instead of diagnosing it. If two teams forked your components, the answer is not "they should have asked". It is "here is what made forking the rational choice, and here is what I changed".

Pass criteria for the defense:
- [ ] All six parts presented within the time
- [ ] Adoption numbers are real and include the bad ones
- [ ] At least one diagnosis given instead of a defense
- [ ] Your reviewer's notes committed in `defense-feedback.md`

Sprint 4 constraint cards: the system must support a fourth product with a completely different brand. One team refuses to adopt and has support from a senior manager (executive backing). You must cut the component count in half. The engineering team will only consume tokens, not components. The system must ship to an external partner with no support channel.
