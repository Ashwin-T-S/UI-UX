## 2. Why We Are Doing It

A working product designer in India cannot rely only on opinions in a meeting. You need evidence from real people who use or try to use the product. This week matters because it turns five conversations into patterns, a revised problem statement, and a portfolio case study with real evidence. It also protects you from the most common research failure: hearing what you hoped to hear.

## 3. What to Learn First

By the end of the week you can:

- Run a 45 minute interview without leading the participant.
- Sit in silence for five seconds and let someone finish thinking.
- Transcribe your own interview and identify every mistake you made in it.
- Turn five conversations into patterns without inventing what you wanted to hear.

Core ideas to review first:

- The interview guide supports you. It is not a script.
- Silence is data. Count to five before you speak.
- A leading question hints at the answer you want.
- A yes or no question closes the story. Ask "walk me through what you did" instead.
- Self-critique means marking mistakes by type. It is a skill, not a judgement.
- A pattern needs a denominator. "3 of 4" is a finding. "Users" is not.
- Keep three things apart: what they said, what they did, and what you infer.
- An affinity map groups observations by topic, not by person.
- A confidence level is high, medium, or low, with a reason.
- Gulf of evaluation: the difficulty of understanding the system's feedback and deciding whether your goal was met.
- AI can invent patterns. L-AI2 helps you catch this.

#### Task - Self-critique (ID `R4.2`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You transcribe part of your interview and mark every mistake you made. Then you write three fixes for interview 2.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
You cannot fix what you cannot see. Counting mistakes shows your real habits, not your intentions.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Learn the five mistake codes: L for leading question, Y for yes or no question, F for filled a silence, P for pitched your own idea, M for missed follow up.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
- Interviewing Users, Ch. 4 "Documenting the Interview".
- Your own interview 1 recording.
- Your R4-1 notes.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Transcribe at least 10 minutes of interview 1 verbatim.
2. Include your own words.
3. Include "um".
4. Mark every leading question with L.
5. Mark every yes or no question with Y.
6. Mark every filled silence with F.
7. Mark every time you pitched or explained your own idea with P.
8. Mark every missed follow up with M.
9. Count each type.
10. Write three fixes for interview 2 as concrete behaviours.

Worked example, a marked fragment:

> Me: So was that part confusing? [L, Y]
> P1: Um, yeah, I guess a bit.
> Me: Right, because a lot of people say the wording there is unclear. [P]
> P1: Actually it wasn't the wording, I just didn't have the...
> Me: The document? [F, and I finished her sentence]
> P1: ...yeah.
>
> What I lost: she was about to tell me what she did not have, and it may not have been a document. I suggested a word she did not say.
>
> Counts: L 1, Y 1, F 1, P 1, M 0
> Fixes for interview 2: 1) After every answer, count to five before speaking. 2) Never complete a sentence. 3) Replace "was it confusing" with "walk me through what you did".

How to think about it: you are not judging yourself. You are finding repeatable behaviours to change.

If you get stuck: listen to one minute again and mark only L and F. Search "leading questions user interview examples". Ask in Discord with your counts and one marked quote.

#### Task - First pass at patterns and critique (ID `R4.6`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You look across all completed interviews and list patterns. Each pattern gets a denominator based on the number of interviews you completed. Then you bring two findings and one quote to critique.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
A pattern with no number is weak. This task teaches you to separate evidence from inference before you write a hypothesis.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Read the synthesis and analysis sections in Just Enough Research. Review the difference between what someone said, what they did, and what you infer.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
- Just Enough Research, synthesis and analysis sections.
- NNGroup, "Affinity Diagramming".
- Your R3.2 risk ranking.
- All completed interview notes.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Read all completed interviews.
2. Find things that recurred.
3. For each pattern, count how many of the completed interviews it appeared in.
4. Always write the denominator, for example "3 of 4".
5. Make one column for what they said.
6. Make one column for what they did.
7. Make one column for what you infer.
8. Keep the three columns apart.
9. Mark anything from only one interview as a single instance.
10. Keep it labelled as n=1.
11. State whether any evidence contradicted your expectations; if none did, say so.
12. Prepare two findings and one quote for critique.

Critique, 90 minutes: present two findings and one quote. The critique focus: is this a finding, or is it your hypothesis with a quote added?

How to think about it: a single instance can be important. It is still not a pattern. Say both things clearly.

If you get stuck: re-read one interview and list only what the person did. Search "how to report qualitative findings with sample size". Ask in Discord with one pattern and its denominator.

### Group 3 - Synthesis and revised hypothesis

#### Task - Affinity map (ID `R4.7`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You turn observations into an affinity map. Each note is one observation. Groups are named with a full sentence.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
A good affinity map shows patterns you can defend. It stops you from grouping by person or by your first idea.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Re-read your P5.2 reframed brief before you look at this week's patterns again. Write three lines on what the evidence supported, changed, or left uncertain. Then continue.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
- Your P5.2 reframed brief.
- Your R4-6 patterns.
- NNGroup, "Affinity Diagramming".
- FigJam or paper. FigJam counts toward free limits.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Write one observation per note.
2. Do not write one participant per note.
3. Group notes by what the observation is about.
4. Do not group by which participant said it.
5. Name each group with a sentence, not a label.
6. Replace a label such as "Documents" with a full sentence you fill from your own notes: "[Who] does not know [what] when [situation]."
7. Count group sizes.
8. Keep the denominators, using the number of completed interviews.
9. Photograph or export the map.
10. Write a plain list of the groups.

If you get stuck: start with 10 notes only. Search "affinity mapping one observation per note". Ask in Discord with one group name and why it is a sentence.

#### Task - Revised hypothesis (ID `P6.1`)

##### 1. What We Are Doing
You rewrite your problem statement using what five people actually told you. You state your confidence and what you still do not know.

##### 2. Why We Are Doing It
The hypothesis is the bridge from research to design. It must show evidence, not only your opinion.

##### 3. What to Learn First
Review the difference between a problem statement and a solution idea. A problem statement says what is hard and for whom. A solution idea says what to build.

##### 4. Where to Find Resources
- Your R4-6 patterns.
- Your R4-7 affinity map.
- Your P5.2 reframed brief.
- Your R3.2 risk ranking.

##### 5. Step-by-Step Guide
1. Rewrite your problem statement using what participants said or did.
2. State your confidence: high, medium, or low.
3. Give a reason for that level.
4. List what you still do not know.
5. Address the brief's original claim directly.
6. If the original claim survived, say so.
7. If it did not survive, say what changed.
8. Name one piece of additional evidence you need.
9. Say how you would check access to it: who to ask, what to request, what you can check without access.

Confidence template:

> Confidence: [high / medium / low].
> Reason: [x of N completed interviews] showed [pattern]. This sample came from [recruitment channel], so it may [limitation].
> What I still do not know: [list].
> Additional evidence I need: [one piece of evidence].
> How I would check access to it: [who to ask, what to request, what I can check without access].

Fill every bracket with your own evidence. Do not copy an example.

How to think about it: Report what the evidence supports, even when it agrees with the original brief.

If you get stuck: write one sentence with "we heard" and one with "we infer". Search "how to state confidence in qualitative research". Ask in Discord with your confidence level and reason.

- [ ] Confidence level stated with a reason for that level

- [ ] The original claim addressed directly, confirmed or refuted

- [ ] How you would check access to it stated
